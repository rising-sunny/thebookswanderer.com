# A 12- or 26-Book Reading Challenge: Two Flexible Plans

Adapt the 52-prompt reading challenge to 12 or 26 books with two practical schedules, a simple reading log and room to change your plans.

## Choose an annual target, not an entire year of books

Start with the number of books you would like to finish, then make a much smaller decision: which three prompts you want to try next. Your annual target and your short reading list serve different purposes. The target gives you a direction; the next three prompts give you something manageable to choose from today.

For a 12-book version, plan around one finished book per month. For a 26-book version, think roughly one every two weeks. These are planning guides rather than deadlines for individual books. You do not need to select all 12 or 26 prompts in January, and you can change the annual target if your circumstances change.

## Set up a four-column reading log

Use a notebook, a spreadsheet or the printable supplied with the original challenge. Keep one row for each finished book and four columns: prompt, title, author and finishing date. If you want a fifth column, use it for a brief personal note rather than another target to meet.

For example, an entry might read: a book you own but never opened; the title from your shelf; its author; 28 January. The date records what happened. It is not a deadline you must predict before starting. Put unfinished possibilities in a separate note so your completed-book count stays clear.

## Keep the next three choices provisional

Choose three prompts from the full list and write a possible book beside each one. Start with books you already own or can borrow. Check the edition if the prompt depends on something physical, such as a map or a page count. Leave the rest of the prompt list available for later.

You can read those three in any order, replace a possibility or choose a new prompt when a recommendation interests you. If a finished book fits several prompts, assign it to one only. For this challenge, each completed prompt represents a different finished book.

## Give the year a rhythm without filling every slot

For a monthly plan, review your next three choices at the start of January, April, July and October. At each review, keep any unfinished choice you still want to read, replace any that no longer appeals and choose a few new possibilities. A quarterly check is a suggestion for organizing the list, not an extra challenge rule.

For a 26-book plan, begin with two possible finishes per month. That accounts for 24 books; leave two additional finishes unassigned until you can see where they fit. Alternatively, use a rough two-week rhythm and ignore month boundaries. Pick one approach for your log so you are not juggling two competing schedules.

## Adjust after a slow or interrupted month

Suppose you planned one finish in January but your book is still unfinished in February. Carry it forward if you want to continue. Then choose whether to keep the annual target, reduce it or leave the decision until your next quarterly review. Avoid turning the missed month into an automatic requirement to finish two books next month.

A long book can still fill one prompt, such as the over-500-pages option, without being assigned an arbitrary finishing week. If you would like a shorter next read, the under-200-pages prompt is another possibility. Choose it because you want to read that book, rather than treating a short book as a penalty for falling behind.

## Use the full prompt list when you need another idea

The two examples below illustrate how to start. They are optional schedules, not official assignments of prompts to months. The full challenge remains your source of alternatives, including translated books, library finds, rereads and books you have been saving.

At the end of the year, count the books you actually finished and look back at your notes. Keep any unused prompts that still interest you, or choose different ones next time. Your log should show your reading year as it happened, including changes of plan.

## Example: your first three months on a 12-book plan

January: choose a book you own but never opened and write its title beside that prompt. February: consider a book translated from another language. March: keep a book under 200 pages as a possible next choice. These three selections are provisional: you can swap their order, replace one or carry a book into the following month. Leave April to December unfilled for now. At the April review, record what you finished and select your next three possibilities. This makes the annual target 12 books while the active plan stays at only three prompts.

## Example: starting a 26-book plan with flexible extras

Begin January with two possibilities: a book recommended by a friend and a book with a map inside. Keep a third possibility, such as a reread of a childhood favourite, ready for the next selection. When you finish one, record it and choose another prompt; do not draw up all 26 in advance. If you prefer monthly planning, two finishes in each of 12 months would total 24, so leave two extra finishes for times when you want another book. The extras are flexible, not obligations for particular holiday months. If a book takes longer, adjust your next choices or your annual target instead of assigning the same book to two prompts.
