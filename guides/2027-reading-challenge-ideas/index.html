A list of prompts is easy to love in December and hard to use in February. “A book with a map inside” is a fine idea until you are standing at a shelf with no notion of what has one. So here is a book for every prompt in our 2027 reading challenge that can have one, and a way in for the rest.

There are 99 books below. We chose ones that fit their prompt properly, that most libraries can lend you, and that mix old and new, short and long. No title appears twice, so you can treat the list as a plan without one book filling two boxes. They are suggestions, not a syllabus. If you have read one, take the next, or ignore the lot.

How to use this list

Do not try to plan all 52. Pick three prompts, look at the suggestions, choose one and start. When you finish it, choose again. That is how the challenge is meant to be used, and it leaves room for the book a friend hands you in April.

The prompts keep the wording and numbers of the free list on the challenge page. Here they are grouped by kind, which makes it easier to find a short book or a poem when you need one.

Cannot choose? Let chance decide.

Only you can answer these

Prompts that depend on you, your shelf or your town. Each has a way in.

  1. 1

    Published the year you were born

    Search for your birth year plus “in literature”. Wikipedia keeps a page for each year, and library catalogues can filter by publication date.

  2. 8

    Recommended by a friend

    Ask one person for one book, not a list. A list gives you an excuse.

  3. 12

    A book you own but never opened

    Look at the shelf you walk past. The one you bought on impulse and then felt faintly guilty about is the answer.

  4. 13

    Chosen only by its cover

    Stand in a section you never visit, give yourself five minutes, and pick by the cover alone. No back-cover blurbs.

  5. 23

    By an author who shares your initials

    Search a library catalogue by your initials. If you are J. A., Jane Austen is waiting. If you are C. D., it is Charles Dickens.

  6. 29

    A reread of a childhood favourite

    Choose the one you remember by feeling rather than by plot. If you cannot remember what happens, that is the one.

  7. 30

    Published this year

    Check the new-releases table in a bookshop or the library's recent arrivals. If you are reading this early in the year, mark the prompt and come back for the spring catalogues.

  8. 36

    A book a stranger was reading

    This one needs a train, a cafe and a little nerve. Ask what it is, and whether they would recommend it.

  9. 38

    Fewer than 1,000 ratings online

    Small presses are a good hunting ground. Sort a shelf on a book site by number of ratings and start from the bottom.

  10. 44

    From a used bookshop

    Go in, set a ten-minute timer, and buy the first thing that surprises you.

  11. 45

    Gifted to you

    If nobody has given you a book lately, ask for one. Or look at the gift you put aside and never read.

  12. 48

    Has an animal on the cover

    Check your own shelves first. You probably own several.

  13. 50

    Picked from the library at random

    Pick a shelf number and a position before you go in: the third shelf, the twelfth book. No peeking at the one next to it.

  14. 51

    A book you'll gift to someone afterwards

    Pick one you would put your name in. For more ideas on what to give a reader, see our gift guide for book lovers.

  15. 52

    The one you've been saving

    Save it for December, when the other fifty-one are done. Then read it.

Questions

How do I choose a book for each reading challenge prompt?

Start with three prompts, not 52. Read the suggestions, pick one that appeals, and choose the next three when you finish. Prompts you cannot imagine yet can wait.

Can one book count for two prompts?

In our challenge, choose one box for each book, so that every completed prompt represents a different book. The suggestions above never repeat a title for that reason.

Do I have to read the prompts in order?

No. Read them in any order.

Do I have to read 52 books?

No. Fifty-two is the full list, not a requirement. A 12-book or 26-book version works too: choose the prompts you want and keep one book per completed prompt.

Do audiobooks and ebooks count?

Yes. Choose the format that suits you: a printed book, an ebook or an audiobook. Library books and books you already own count too.

What if I have already read the suggested books?

Take another from the same prompt, or ask a librarian for a book that fits it. The suggestions are starting points, not the only answers.
