How to read without holding the book

A practical guide · November 2026 · Leer en español

Holding a book open for an hour is tiring, and it gets in the way of a cup of tea, a sandwich or a sleeping cat. The answer depends on where you read: at a table, on the sofa or in bed. Here is what works in each place, from free tricks to the stands we sell.

If you only need one hand free, start with our guide on how to hold a book open with one hand. This one is about not holding it at all.

For most people the answer is a book stand plus a page clip. The stand takes the weight and sets the angle, and the clip stops the pages from turning on their own. It works at a desk, a kitchen counter or a table in bed.

At a table or desk

This is the easiest place to read hands-free. Put the book on a stand that tilts it towards you, so you are not bending your neck over a flat page, and clip the pages that want to close. A folding stand with several angles also works for cookbooks and textbooks.

No gear? Lay the book flat and rest something heavy and clean along the bottom margin of each side: another book on its side, a phone in a case or a glasses case. Keep the weight on the margin, not across the text.

On the sofa or in an armchair

Your lap is not flat, so a book slides and tips. A lap stand gives it a base: the book leans back at a reading angle and your hands are free for a mug. A cushion under the stand raises it closer to your eyes.

In bed

Bed is the hardest place, because most of us read lying on our side or propped up on pillows, and a paper book has to be held up. Three things help:

  • Sit up and use a stand. With pillows behind you, a lap stand on the duvet works like it does on the sofa. A wedge pillow (a firm, triangular back rest) makes this more comfortable.
  • Switch to an e-reader for bedtime. It weighs about as much as a paperback but stays open by itself, and with a page-turner remote you can keep your hands under the blanket and still turn pages.
  • Light the page, not the room. A light worn around the neck points at the book and not at the person next to you.

What about book weights, wedges and slings?

You will see these names when you search:

  • Book weight (or book anchor): a weighted strip or small bag that lies across the bottom of the pages. Good at a table, useless on your lap.
  • Book wedge: usually a wedge-shaped pillow that props you up in bed, not something for the book itself.
  • Book sling: a strap or cradle that holds the book while you lie down. Handy for a heavy hardback, but you still have to turn the pages by hand.

We don't sell these; the stand-plus-clip setup does the same job at a table, and an e-reader does it better in bed.

Questions

How can I keep a book open without holding it?

At a table, put it on a book stand and clip the pages. On the sofa, use a lap stand. In bed, sit up with a lap stand on the duvet, or switch to an e-reader with a page-turner remote.

How do I keep a new paperback from closing?

Weigh down the bottom margins at a table, or use a page clip. Avoid pressing the spine flat: it cracks the glue and pages can come loose.

What is the best way to read in bed without holding a book?

An e-reader stays open by itself; with a page-turner remote you can keep your hands under the covers. For paper books, sit up and use a lap stand, and a neck light so the room stays dark.

Does a reading stand work with heavy hardcovers?

Yes. A stand with locking angles holds a heavy hardback, and its weight helps it stay open. Add a clip for the first and last pages, where the book pulls hardest.
