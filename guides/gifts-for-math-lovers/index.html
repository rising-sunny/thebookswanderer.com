What the equations mean, in one line each

  • Euler's identity, eiπ + 1 = 0 links the five most important numbers in mathematics in one short line, which is why it is so often called the most beautiful equation.
  • Fourier series say that any repeating signal, even a square wave, is a sum of simple sine waves. It is the idea behind MP3s, JPEGs and every equaliser.
  • The wave equation describes how a guitar string, a drumhead or light vibrates. Its standing modes are the pattern on the bookmark.
  • The Dirac equation joined quantum mechanics and special relativity for the electron, and in doing so predicted antimatter before anyone had seen it.
  • The Navier–Stokes equations describe how air and water flow. Proving that their solutions always behave is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems, with a one-million-dollar reward still unclaimed.
  • The Mandelbrot set comes from repeating z → z² + c. A single line of arithmetic produces an edge that never stops getting more detailed.
